\section*{1. Executive Summary}
The Municipal Corporation needs data-driven interventions to reduce congestion and improve transit planning. We analyze the NYC Taxi Trip Duration dataset with \textbf{1,458,644} trips from \textbf{January 1, 2016 to June 30, 2016}. The dataset has 11 features, including pickup/dropoff timestamps, GPS coordinates, passenger count, and trip duration.

We formulate three linked mining tasks: (1) \textit{trip duration prediction}, (2) \textit{spatial demand clustering}, and (3) \textit{congestion-level classification}. Together, these support operational planning, infrastructure prioritization, and policy design.

\section*{2. Data Exploration Summary}
The data is complete (no missing values) and temporally consistent (no pickup-after-dropoff records; no mismatch between computed and provided durations). Profiling shows strong right-skew in trip duration and clear time-of-day effects in demand and speed.

\textbf{Data quality findings}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
    \item Coordinates outside NYC bounds (40.4--41.0 N, -74.3 to -73.7 W): \textbf{985 trips} (0.0675\%); invalid pickups: 247 and invalid dropoffs: 971.
    \item Duration outliers include \textbf{8,595} short trips ($<$60 s, 0.5892\%) and \textbf{4} extreme long trips ($>$24 h).
    \item Passenger-count anomalies are limited: \textbf{60} zero-passenger trips and \textbf{5} trips with more than 6 passengers.
    \item Derived-metric checks show \textbf{175} speed anomalies ($>$100 km/h) and \textbf{5,897} zero-distance trips.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Notable patterns}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt]
    \item Demand peaks during morning and evening commute windows.
    \item Median speed declines during those peak windows.
    \item Spatial demand concentrates in core Manhattan and airport corridors.
\end{itemize}

\section*{3. Problem Formulation}
\textbf{Task 1: Trip Duration Prediction}\\
\textit{Mathematical task:} Supervised Regression\\
\textit{Input $\rightarrow$ Output:} Temporal features (hour, weekday, month), pickup/dropoff coordinates, vendor/passenger fields, and Haversine distance $\rightarrow$ predicted trip duration (seconds).\\
\textit{Business value:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
    \item Travel-time forecasting for route planning and passenger information systems.
    \item Incident flagging when observed duration significantly exceeds model prediction.
    \item Dynamic bus/subway capacity planning on parallel high-load corridors.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Planned models and evaluation:} Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting; performance reported with RMSE/MAE and error breakdown by time-of-day.

\textbf{Task 2: High-Demand Zone Identification}\\
\textit{Mathematical task:} Unsupervised Clustering\\
\textit{Input $\rightarrow$ Output:} Pickup coordinates (optionally enriched with demand counts and mean duration) $\rightarrow$ cluster labels for geographic demand zones.\\
\textit{Business value:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
    \item Identify underserved high-demand areas for transit augmentation.
    \item Prioritize local interventions (signal timing, lane policy, and curb management).
    \item Guide investment decisions such as bike-share, park-and-ride, and zone planning.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Planned models and evaluation:} K-Means \textbf{vs.} DBSCAN (required algorithm comparison); quality assessed using Silhouette Score and cluster interpretability.

\textbf{Task 3: Congestion Level Classification}\\
\textit{Mathematical task:} Supervised Multi-class Classification\\
\textit{Input $\rightarrow$ Output:} Zone ID, hour, weekday, historical trip count, and average speed $\rightarrow$ congestion class \{High, Medium, Low\}. Labels are derived using speed-percentile thresholds across zone--time buckets.\\
\textit{Business value:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
    \item Dynamic transit service-frequency control by zone and time bucket.
    \item Commuter-facing congestion alerts, route suggestions, and ETA guidance.
    \item Operations and staffing planning with evidence for policy interventions.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Planned models and evaluation:} Logistic Regression and Random Forest Classifier; performance measured using Accuracy, macro-F1, and class-wise error analysis.
